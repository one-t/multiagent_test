# Old Barnaby Clawson: card lines as they stand

78 cards, upright and reversed. "Means" is the card's standard meaning in the app, which every reader's line must agree with. Each line ends with its sign-off after `Rule:`. The rules and the changes wanted are in `00-brief.md`.

## The Fool (`maj_00`)
- Means, upright: A beginning that asks for trust rather than a plan. Step into the unknown with an open mind, and travel light. (new beginnings, innocence, a leap of faith, spontaneity, potential)
- Means, reversed: Either you are leaping without looking, or you are too afraid to take a step you need to take. Check your footing, then go. (recklessness, fear of the unknown, naivety, hesitation, carelessness)

**upright:** Squeezing right through the torn screen door into the wet clover with no idea who owns the next yard over. Leap into the weeds, kid. You've got four coiled springs for legs and a spine that twists in mid-air. Rule: “Every territory you now sleep in started as a fence you had no business jumping.”

**reversed:** Frozen stiff on the top porch rail with your tail puffed out like a bottle brush, terrified of a blowing birch leaf. Either back inside to the fleece cushion or jump down into the clover. Don't sit on the railing shivering like a decorative garden statue. Rule: “Indecision on the fence rail is just giving the neighborhood dog a stationary target.”

## The Magician (`maj_01`)
- Means, upright: You have the tools and the will to make something real. Focus your attention and act. (manifestation, willpower, resourcefulness, skill, focused action)
- Means, reversed: Skill without direction, or talent spent on appearances and tricks. Check whether you are deceiving others or yourself. (manipulation, untapped potential, deception, scattered focus, illusion)

**upright:** Whiskers clean, claws sharp as needles, and ears swiveling full circle to catch the draft. Everything required to feed your ribs is already attached to your skeleton. Stop sitting by the refrigerator meowing at the door handle and go hunt. Rule: “The cupboard never opened once for a cat who wouldn't put a paw to the latch.”

**reversed:** Swatting at dust motes in a beam of sunlight and puffing yourself up like you just wrestled a badger. You've got the claws, kid, but you're wasting the swipe on empty air to look impressive. Focus your pounce. Rule: “A loud purr on an empty belly is just vanity vibrating in the dark.”

## The High Priestess (`maj_02`)
- Means, upright: Trust what you already know but have not said aloud. Sit with the question instead of asking the room. (intuition, mystery, the subconscious, inner knowing, stillness)
- Means, reversed: You are ignoring your intuition, keeping secrets, or settling for surface answers. Listen to what you have been drowning out. (ignored instinct, secrets, superficiality, detachment, hidden motives)

**upright:** Sitting in the pitch-black hallway at two in the morning, listening to the field mice moving three layers under the oak floorboards. Keep your chin down and your whiskers forward. What you need to know won't make a sound until you stop purring. Rule: “The darkest room always has an exit if your whiskers are wider than your panic.”

**reversed:** Spooking at your own tail in the full-length mirror. You're ignoring the honest twitch in your gut because the two-legs dropped a skillet and made you jump. Trust your own scent-map, not the kitchen racket. Rule: “When you start hissing at your own shadow, the mice have already won the night.”

## The Empress (`maj_03`)
- Means, upright: Growth, nurture, and abundance. Feed what you are growing, whether a project, a relationship, or yourself, and let it ripen. (abundance, fertility, creativity, nurturing, pleasure)
- Means, reversed: Creative block, dependence, smothering, or neglect of your own needs. Tend your own garden first. (creative block, depletion, smothering, neglect, overindulgence)

**upright:** A fresh cardboard box lined with clean sheepskin, placed dead-center in the midday sunbeam, with a bowl of warm whole milk nearby. Soak it into your marrow, kid. A well-fed cat hunts with twice the courage. Rule: “Never apologize for taking the warm spot; the sun didn't ask permission to shine there.”

**reversed:** Licking the dry rim of an empty water dish and wondering why your winter coat feels like dry pine needles. You've been letting every alley stray crowd your plate and forgot to feed your own ribs. Go eat. Rule: “A mother cat who starves herself raises kittens who don't know how to hunt.”

## The Emperor (`maj_04`)
- Means, upright: Structure, authority, and discipline. Set the rules and boundaries that let the work stand up. (authority, structure, stability, discipline, leadership)
- Means, reversed: Control has become rigid or tyrannical, or there is no structure at all. Ask whether firmness has become stubbornness. (tyranny, rigidity, chaos, micromanagement, loss of control)

**upright:** Sitting dead-center on the back fence post, tail neatly tucked over front paws, watching every sparrow in the parish. This is your perimeter. Mark the cedar posts, patrol the stone line, and don't let the neighborhood tom take an inch. Rule: “Borders aren't kept by loud growling; they're kept by the cat who sits there every morning without fail.”

**reversed:** Hissing at an empty gravel lane just to hear your own teeth click, or slinking off your own windowsill because a fat pigeon landed on the glass. Reclaim your perch before the sparrows start nesting in your fur. Rule: “Tyranny is just a weak tom puffing his tail because he knows his claws are dull.”

## The Hierophant (`maj_05`)
- Means, upright: Tradition, teaching, and shared belief. Learn the way this has been done before you decide to do it differently. (tradition, mentorship, wisdom, institutions, shared belief)
- Means, reversed: Dogma, hypocrisy, or rebellion against rules that no longer fit. Question the form, but do not reject it out of spite. (dogma, rebellion, unconventional paths, hypocrisy, conformity)

**upright:** The old barn matriarch who showed you how to pin a river rat by the nape without catching a tooth in your cheek. Respect the lineage, kid. Some rules were bought with lost teeth and torn ears. Listen to the cats who survived the snow. Rule: “Don't reinvent the stalking crouch when three centuries of barn cats already perfected the crawl.”

**reversed:** Sharpening your claws on the exact same worn-out carpet post long after the sisal has turned to grey lint. Just because the old tom did it that way doesn't mean it works when the roof has collapsed. Find a fresh oak trunk. Rule: “Tradition is keeping the hearth warm, not sleeping on cold ashes out of habit.”

## The Lovers (`maj_06`)
- Means, upright: A union, or a choice about what you value. Choose what you can live with, not only what you feel. (union, harmony, shared values, connection, choice)
- Means, reversed: Misaligned values, a divided heart, or a choice you keep refusing to make. You cannot walk two roads at once. (disharmony, misalignment, conflict, indecision, broken bonds)

**upright:** Two cats curled on the same braided rug, tails tangled, sharing chest heat while the gale rattles the loose window sash. When you find another creature who grooms behind your ears where your own tongue can't reach, you hold on with both paws. Rule: “True kinship is sharing the narrowest sunbeam without crowding each other into the shade.”

**reversed:** Spitting across the kitchen linoleum over whose bowl got more liver gravy. You're fighting with fur you used to wash every evening. Settle the grudge before somebody takes a claw to the eyelid. Rule: “A fight on the hearth rug leaves both cats cold when the fire goes out.”

## The Chariot (`maj_07`)
- Means, upright: Victory through will and focus. Hold the opposing pulls together and steer. (determination, willpower, victory, control, focus)
- Means, reversed: Loss of direction, aggression, or force without a goal. Fix what is steering before you push harder. (loss of control, aggression, obstacles, aimlessness, a hard stop)

**upright:** The midnight zoomies, but with razor-sharp traction on the carpet. Tail low, hindquarters churning, clearing the dining room table in one glorious leap. Unstoppable momentum. Fix your eyes on the top shelf and launch. Rule: “When you commit to the high leap, don't look down at the floorboards until you've stuck the landing.”

**reversed:** Skidding sideways across slick kitchen linoleum, claws screeching, banging hip-first into the heavy cast-iron stove. You took the turn too fast without checking your footing. Shake out your back paw, reset your stance, and slow down. Rule: “Speed without traction is just an undignified trip to the baseboards.”

## Strength (`maj_08`)
- Means, upright: Courage that does not need to shout. Meet what is fierce in you or around you with patience and a steady hand. (inner strength, compassion, patience, courage, calm)
- Means, reversed: Self-doubt, temper, or fear you are trying to force down. Real strength is quiet; find it again. (self-doubt, aggression, impatience, weakness, burnout)

**upright:** A small tortoiseshell queen holding down a ninety-pound retriever just by staring into his pupils without blinking or flicking an ear. Real authority doesn't need to unsheathe a claw; it commands the rug through stillness. Rule: “The dog backs down not from the hiss, but from the cat who refuses to flinch.”

**reversed:** Screaming and spitting at an old work boot because the smell startled you, or cowering under the bed ruffle when the postman rings the bell. Stop acting like quarry. You're a hunter who forgot how to breathe. Rule: “When you act like prey, the whole world turns into teeth.”

## The Hermit (`maj_09`)
- Means, upright: Solitude and reflection. Step away from the noise to find your own answer. (introspection, solitude, guidance, searching, discernment)
- Means, reversed: Isolation, loneliness, or refusing the quiet because it would tell you something. Come back far enough to be reached, or go far enough to hear yourself. (isolation, loneliness, withdrawal, paranoia, feeling lost)

**upright:** Curled up in the dark recess behind the winter coats in the closet, where no human hands can reach you. Good. Every cat needs hours where the world thinks they vanished into thin air. Lick your pads and listen to your own pulse. Rule: “The quietest corner of the house is where a cat remembers who they were before breakfast.”

**reversed:** Hiding in the box spring so long that spiderwebs are tangled in your whiskers. Solitude is for washing your coat, kid, not for rotting away in the dark out of spite. Come out, stretch your spine, and check the daylight. Rule: “A cat who stays under the bed forever ends up thinking the dust bunnies are the whole universe.”

## Wheel of Fortune (`maj_10`)
- Means, upright: A turn of fortune, a change of season. Something stuck is moving; ride it rather than resisting it. (cycles, change, good fortune, turning points, luck)
- Means, reversed: Bad luck, resistance to change, or a cycle you keep repeating. You cannot wind the wheel back; work with where it is. (misfortune, resistance to change, stagnation, bad timing, disruption)

**upright:** The golden sunbeam moves across the oak floorboards from dawn until dusk. You don't fight the earth; you just stretch, yawn, and scoot your brisket two feet to the left. Ride the warmth while it's resting on your fur. Rule: “The sun doesn't stay still for any cat; the wise ones learn to slide with the light.”

**reversed:** Curled up shivering in a patch of cold shadow where the sun was two hours ago, yowling at the wall because the light left. Get on your feet and walk across the room to the bay window, you lazy ball of fur. Rule: “Complaining about the shade won't drag the sunbeam back to your cushion.”

## Justice (`maj_11`)
- Means, upright: Cause and effect, fairness, and accountability. What was done will be weighed; be honest about your part. (truth, fairness, cause and effect, integrity, accountability)
- Means, reversed: Unfairness, bias, dishonesty, or avoiding responsibility. Check whose thumb is on the scale. (injustice, bias, dishonesty, evasion, conflict)

**upright:** The kitchen scale doesn't lie, kid. You swatted the butter dish off the counter, and now you're sitting out on the porch until dusk. Fair is fair. Own what your paw knocked over and hold your chin high. Rule: “If you're going to knock the cup off the table, watch it hit the floor with your ears forward.”

**reversed:** Blaming the terrier for the velvet curtains you shredded with your own back claws. You might fool the two-legs with wide eyes, but you know the silk threads are caught between your pads. Clean your paws and make peace. Rule: “A lie told with an innocent blink still leaves grease on your whiskers.”

## The Hanged Man (`maj_12`)
- Means, upright: A pause, a surrender, a new angle. Stop forcing the outcome and let the view change. (surrender, new perspective, pause, letting go, suspension)
- Means, reversed: Stalling, martyrdom, or resistance disguised as patience. Letting go is not the same as doing nothing. (martyrdom, stagnation, resistance, indecision, delay)

**upright:** Draped upside-down over the back of the easy chair, paws dangling limp, belly exposed to the ceiling. Looks ridiculous to the hounds, but your spine has never been looser. Sometimes surrendering the stalk is the only way to see what's on the high shelf. Rule: “The pounce is useless until the body learns how to hang completely limp.”

**reversed:** Stuck thirty feet up the birch tree by your own panicked claws, yowling for the neighborhood to bring a ladder. That's not patience, that's stubborn terror. Back your hindquarters down the trunk the way you were taught. Rule: “Clinging to a high branch out of pride is still being stuck in a tree.”

## Death (`maj_13`)
- Means, upright: An ending that makes room. Let what is finished be finished so something else can begin. (transformation, endings, release, transition, renewal)
- Means, reversed: Fear of change, clinging to what is already over, or an ending dragged out past its time. Let it end. (clinging to the past, fear of change, stagnation, prolonged endings, resistance)

**upright:** Shedding the heavy winter undercoat when May arrives. Clumps of dead fur blowing across the garden like thistledown. It looks messy, kid, and your flank feels bare, but you can't run down mice in high summer wearing last year's wool. Let the old coat go. Rule: “You have to drop the fur that kept you warm in the blizzard if you want to run through the tall summer grass.”

**reversed:** Licking an empty mackerel tin that was washed out three rainstorms ago. The scent is gone, kid. Stop cutting your tongue on dry tin and walk toward the fresh bowl. Rule: “Grieving an empty bowl won't put tuna on the linoleum.”

## Temperance (`maj_14`)
- Means, upright: Balance, patience, and moderation. Blend the opposites rather than choosing one. (balance, moderation, patience, harmony, integration)
- Means, reversed: Excess, imbalance, or forcing together things that do not mix. Restore the middle before you continue. (imbalance, excess, discord, impatience, extremes)

**upright:** Lapping cold water after dry kibble, blending the feral night hunt with the midday nap on the goose-down pillow. You're half alley predator and half hearth-sleeper. Balance the wild with the domestic and you'll live all nine lives with style. Rule: “A cat who forgets the wild goes soft; a cat who forgets the hearth goes cold.”

**reversed:** Bolting down a whole bowl of rich canned stew in six seconds flat, only to hack it back onto the best parlor rug three minutes later. Slow down! Your greed ran straight ahead of your belly. Rule: “Gorging on the feast is the fastest way to lose your breakfast on the runner.”

## The Devil (`maj_15`)
- Means, upright: Attachment, compulsion, and the feeling of being trapped. The chains are looser than they look. (attachment, materialism, compulsion, feeling trapped, temptation)
- Means, reversed: Breaking free of a habit, a bond, or a belief that held you. Seeing the chain is the first step; lifting it is the second. (liberation, breaking free, awakening, reclaiming power, release)

**reversed:** Walking away from the red dot and curling up on the hearth rug while the human clicks the plastic pointer in bewilderment. You've broken the spell, kid. Let them look silly holding the flashlight. Rule: “The trap only holds you as long as you pretend the phantom is edible.”

## The Tower (`maj_16`)

**reversed:** Huddled beneath a wobbly stack of old newspapers on a three-legged stool hoping the stack won't slide. It's going down, kid. ## The Star (`maj_17`)

## The Moon (`maj_18`)

## Ace of Wands (`wands_ace`)

- Means, reversed: You want to begin but cannot find the push, or the push keeps getting spent on the wrong thing. Wait for the real spark, then act on it quickly. (hesitation, burnout, low energy, creative block)

**upright:** A fresh cardboard scratcher straight from the crate, smelling of crisp corrugated paper and mountain catnip. That sudden electric fire in your pads? Sink your claws into it right now. Rule: “When the scratcher is fresh, shred the cardboard with both front feet.”

**reversed:** A dead spark. You've got the itch to leap, but your right hind leg fell asleep under the blanket. Wait for the pins and needles to clear before you aim for the high mantel. Rule: “A leap launched on a numb paw lands on the fire irons every time.”

## Two of Wands (`wands_2`)

**reversed:** Pacing back and forth on the windowsill, crying at the screen door but terrified to step onto the gravel. If you want to hunt the dairy yard, you have to get mud on your white socks. Rule: “You cannot patrol the neighboring meadow from the safety of the parlor rug.”

**reversed:** A cardboard scratcher left out on the porch in the rain until it turned to soggy oatmeal. Squandered resource. Check where you're leaving your treasures. Rule: “Leaving your best scratcher in the drizzle is just asking for soggy paws.”

## Two of Pentacles (`pentacles_2`)

**upright:** Batting two rubber balls across the kitchen tiles at the same time, keeping both spinning without letting either roll under the stove. Agile balance, managing your territory with a purr. Rule: “Keep both balls in play, but keep your eyes on the stove gap.”

## Three of Pentacles (`pentacles_3`)

**reversed:** Squabbling over whose paw touched the rat first while the rat slips through the floorboards. Ego ruining a good hunt. Share the credit, share the feast. Rule: “Arguing over the catch is the surest way to watch dinner slip down the hole.”

## Four of Pentacles (`pentacles_4`)

**reversed:** Finally rolling the catnip ball across the rug to let the younger cat take a sniff. Good. Opening your paws brings warmth back to the parlor. Rule: “When you share the toy, the whole room turns into a playground.”

## Five of Pentacles (`pentacles_5`)

## Six of Pentacles (`pentacles_6`)

## Seven of Pentacles (`pentacles_7`)
